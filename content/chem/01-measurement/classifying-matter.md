This is the vocabulary everything else sits on. It is mostly definitions, but
the definitions get tested precisely and there are two traps in here worth
spotting now rather than in a quiz.

### The tree

```
                        MATTER
                   /              \
        PURE SUBSTANCE            MIXTURE
         /         \             /        \
   ELEMENT     COMPOUND   HOMOGENEOUS   HETEROGENEOUS
                           (solution)
```

- **Element** - one kind of atom. Copper, oxygen, carbon.
- **Compound** - different elements **chemically bonded in a fixed ratio**.
  Water, salt, carbon dioxide.
- **Homogeneous mixture** - uniform throughout; every sample identical. Also
  called a **solution**. Salt water, air, brass.
- **Heterogeneous mixture** - composition varies from place to place. Sand in
  water, salad dressing, granite.

### Compound or mixture? It comes down to bonding

| | Compound | Mixture |
|---|---|---|
| Bonded? | Yes | No |
| Proportions | **Fixed** | **Any** |
| Separating it | Needs a **chemical reaction** | **Physical** - filter, boil, magnet |

Water is *always* two hydrogens per oxygen. There is no slightly-more-hydrogen
water. Salt water, by contrast, can be barely salty or nearly saturated.

And boiling salt water gets your salt back, while boiling water just gives you
steam - still water. Breaking a compound takes chemistry.

### Trap one: O₂ is an element

Oxygen gas is two atoms **bonded together**, and it is still an **element**,
because both atoms are the same kind. **Element means one kind of atom** - not
one atom, and nothing about bonding.

Same for H₂, N₂, Cl₂, S₈. All elements. A **compound** needs at least two
*different* elements.

### Trap two: dissolving is physical

Sugar in water *looks* like a chemical change - the sugar vanishes. It isn't.
The sugar molecules are completely intact, just dispersed among the water
instead of stacked in a crystal. Evaporate the water and every gram comes back
unchanged.

### Physical or chemical change?

One question settles it: **is there a new substance at the end?**

- **Physical** - same substance, different form. Melting, boiling, dissolving,
  bending, grinding.
- **Chemical** - a new substance exists. Rusting, burning, digesting,
  neutralising.

Hints that suggest a chemical change: colour change, gas given off, a solid
precipitating, heat or light released, a new smell.

**But they are only hints.** Boiling water bubbles vigorously and is purely
physical. A hotplate gets hot without reacting at all. Always come back to the
real question: *new substance, or not?*
